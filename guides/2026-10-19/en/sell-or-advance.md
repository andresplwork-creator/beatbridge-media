Selling your catalog pays the most cash today, but the rights never come back. An advance pays less upfront, but you keep the asset and its income after recoupment. If you are still signed and unrecouped, a buyout may be the better route. The right choice depends on how much cash you need, how old and stable your income is and what you want to own in ten years.

This guide compares the options with simple numbers so you can see what each one really costs you.

## The five ways to raise money on a catalog

| Option | Cash today | What you keep | When it ends | Usually fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full sale | The highest, priced as a multiple of annual net income | Nothing in the rights you sell | Never: the transfer is permanent | A clean exit, estate planning, a large one-off need |
| Partial sale | A share of the full price | The unsold share and its income | Permanent for the share you sell | Cash without giving up everything |
| Distribution licence advance | Lower than a sale, usually higher for longer terms | Ownership of your masters | At the end of a fixed term of 5 to 25 years | Owners of a back catalog who want to keep the asset |
| Royalty advance | Based on income you already receive | Ownership, and often your current deals | When the advance is recouped or the term ends | Artists who want cash without changing deals |
| Unrecouped buyout | Funds the payoff of your label or distributor balance, sometimes with cash on top | Independence and 100% of your catalog for an agreed period, depending on the deal | As agreed case by case | Artists signed to a deal they have not recouped |

Distributors also offer small automated advances on streaming income. We cover those in a separate guide on [distributor advances](/insights/distributor-advances).

## How much each option pays, in rules of thumb

These ranges are public reference points, not offers. Real numbers depend on your statements, contracts and the market at the time.

- **Sale:** buyers price a catalog as a multiple of net annual income. Our [value calculator](/music-catalog-value-calculator?mode=buyout) uses indicative bands of about 4 to 6 times for catalogs under three years old, 5 to 10 times for three to ten years and 8 to 12 times for older catalogs. Public guides put smaller or newer independent catalogs at about 4 to 8 times and blue chip legacy catalogs at 15 to 20 times or more.
- **Advance:** one public comparison puts royalty advances at roughly 1 to 3 times annual earnings. With a licence advance the term matters: a longer term usually supports a larger amount. You can see indicative amounts by term in the [advance mode of the calculator](/music-catalog-value-calculator?mode=advance).

## A worked example

Imagine a catalog that earns 50,000 a year in net income, is eight years old and has stable streams. These figures are illustrative.

| | Full sale | Licence advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash today | Around 250,000 to 500,000 at 5 to 10 times | Around 50,000 to 150,000 at 1 to 3 times |
| Income during the deal | Goes to the buyer | Recovers the advance first, then is shared under the agreed split |
| After the deal | Nothing comes back | The masters return to you, still earning |
| What you hold in ten years | Cash, if you kept it | Cash from the advance plus a catalog you own |

The sale looks bigger, and often it is. But look at what the buyer is paying for. At 7 times income, the buyer pays seven years of today's income upfront. If you believe your catalog will keep earning for much longer than that, keeping it is worth more to you than a sale. If you need a large amount now, or you think income will fall, the sale may be the better deal.

## Which option fits your situation

**You are signed to a label or distributor and still unrecouped.** Most of your income goes to recoup the balance, so you see little of it. A financed [unrecouped buyout](/unrecouped-advance-buyout) can pay off that balance so you leave the deal and own your catalog again. Our step-by-step plan for [leaving a label deal while unrecouped](/insights/leave-label-deal-unrecouped) explains how it works.

**You own your masters and have releases older than two or three years with steady income.** A [distribution licence advance](/distribution-licence-advance) gives you cash and keeps the asset. Compare 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 year terms side by side before deciding.

**You are happy with your label but need capital.** You may not need to leave. Income outside your deal, such as songwriter royalties, neighbouring rights or recordings you own, can support a [royalty advance](/funding-royalty-advance).

**You want a clean exit or need the largest possible amount.** A full or partial sale may fit. Start with [should I sell my music catalog](/insights/should-i-sell-my-music-catalog) and the [selling guide](/sell-your-music-catalog).

**You have a small, newer catalog and need a small amount fast.** A distributor advance can work, but check what control you give up while it is active.

## Five questions to answer before you choose

1. **How much cash do you actually need, and by when?** Ask for the amount you need, not the largest number on offer.
2. **Is your income growing, stable or falling?** Growing income favours keeping the asset. Falling income favours selling sooner.
3. **What will you do with the money?** Money that funds new music or a business can earn more than the catalog. Money for a one-off expense cannot.
4. **Are you free to deal?** Check whether a current label, distributor or publisher contract limits what you can sell, license or advance.
5. **What do you want to own in ten years?** If the answer is "my catalog", a sale is probably not your first option.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an advance better than selling?

Not always. An advance keeps your ownership but pays less today. A sale pays more but is permanent. The better choice depends on how much cash you need and how long you expect the catalog to keep earning.

### Can I get an advance and sell later?

Usually yes. A licence advance or royalty advance does not stop you from selling after the term, and some deals allow an earlier sale with the funder's consent. Check the transfer and buyout clauses before signing.

### Do I have to repay an advance if my music earns less?

In most recoupable advances, no: the money is recovered only from the income covered by the agreement. Some products are loans in practice, so read the recourse clause. Our guide on [types of music advances](/insights/types-of-music-advances) explains the difference.

### How quickly can each option close?

A distributor advance can arrive in days. Licence advances, buyouts and sales take longer because they need a review of statements and contracts, often several weeks to a few months.
